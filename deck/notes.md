# Speaker notes: 3 minutes, about 390 words

Seven slides. Slide 3 is where the live demo happens, so it gets the most time. The pitch ends on the ask. Say only numbers that are in `RESULTS.md` on the day; the ones below were true on Nov 15. Parts in square brackets are for you to decide and are not counted in the 390 words.

## Slide 1. Title (0:00 to 0:15, about 40 words)

Hi, I'm Jithin. I'm a master's student in computer science, and I work as a Robotics Engineer at the Baum Family Maker Space. This is Forge. Describe what you want to build, and it finds where at USC to make it.

## Slide 2. Problem (0:15 to 0:40, about 55 words)

USC has the tools. A mechanical engineering freshman walked into the Maker Space asking for scrap wood. He had a project in mind, and questions. Where can I make this? Am I allowed in? What training do I need? What will it cost? Our staff answer those again and again. Meanwhile, students keep asking the desk for leftover material.

## Slide 3. Product, with the live demo (0:40 to 1:25, about 90 words)

(Phone.) A first-year types one sentence: a wooden sign with the club's name, for the involvement fair. No login.

Forge asks one question, because the size changes the answer. Tap Shoebox.

Here is the plan. Three ways to build it, and the best fit for this student is first. The Maker Space is open to them. These are the trainings to take first, in order.

Now the materials. Staff posted leftover plywood, so the plan offers it free to claim. New, the sheet is an estimated six dollars, so it could cost less.

## Slide 4. Scrap Exchange (1:25 to 1:50, about 50 words)

That free piece comes from the Scrap Exchange. Staff photograph a leftover, the AI drafts the listing, and staff check it before it goes live. A student claims it, and it's held for forty-eight hours. We tested five students claiming one piece at the same instant: exactly one gets it.

## Slide 5. How the AI stays honest (1:50 to 2:20, about 65 words)

How do we keep the AI honest? The model only proposes. Code checks every tool against that space's own data, and rejects the plan if a tool isn't there. The model never sets a price. We ran twenty-one test ideas on the live model: twenty got an accepted space as the top route, and every access badge was right. A hundred and thirty-seven automated checks pass.

## Slide 6. Adoption (2:20 to 2:35, about 35 words)

Forge launches on estimates, and says so. Eight spaces are listed today, each one a single data file. Their own staff sign in, fix what's wrong, and press Confirm. None are confirmed yet. That's the pilot's first job.

## Slide 7. Ask (2:35 to 3:00, about 55 words)

So here's the ask. One pilot semester at the Maker Space. Faculty: give your first-years this link in week one. Staff at other spaces, and student clubs: confirm your page and post your leftovers. Next, and not built yet: research labs would post their leftovers too. It's live now. Scan the code and try it.
